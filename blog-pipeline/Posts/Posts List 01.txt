## LinkedIn Post 6 — Building MDify

A quick architecture note from building **MDify**.

The platform is designed around four processing paths:

**N1 + N2** → normal document processing  
**O1 + O2** → OCR-heavy workloads

Files and generated results are stored through **Supabase Storage**, while job metadata, KPIs and audit data live in **Supabase Postgres**.

The important part isn’t the number of services.

It’s workload separation.

A large OCR task shouldn’t block a normal document conversion.

A complex ZIP shouldn’t launch 1,000 child processes at once.

A scanned PDF shouldn’t force every native-text page through OCR.

The architecture is being optimized around **bounded parallelism and content-aware routing**.

That keeps the user experience simple while the processing layer becomes smarter.

https://markdify.com

## Patreon Post 4 — Architecture Note

**Title:** Why MDify Uses Separate OCR and Normal Processing Pools

OCR and normal document conversion behave very differently.

OCR is CPU-heavy and memory-sensitive.

Normal document extraction is usually lighter and faster.

Instead of forcing them through the same worker, MDify separates them:

**N1 + N2 → normal processing**  
**O1 + O2 → OCR**

That allows the platform to keep normal jobs moving even when OCR workloads are heavy.

It also makes performance testing more realistic because we can measure both pools separately.

That’s the kind of engineering work happening behind what looks like a simple “upload → Markdown” experience.
